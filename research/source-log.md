## 2026-06-21 Energy and Latency Budget Sources

- Frontiers in Physics, "A review of emerging trends in photonic deep learning
  accelerators" (2024).
  URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physics/articles/10.3389/fphy.2024.1369099/full
  Notes: Reports example photonic tensor-core efficiency estimates such as
  5.1 TOPS/W for a 64x64 crossbar-style core, useful as a conservative
  sub-pJ/op reference point.

- Applied Physics Letters, "A 262 TOPS hyperdimensional photonic AI accelerator
  powered by silicon photonics" (2025).
  URL: https://pubs.aip.org/aip/app/article/10/11/110805/3372196/A-262-TOPS-hyperdimensional-photonic-AI
  Notes: Reports a silicon-photonic estimate around 273 fJ/op, used as an
  optimistic optical-operation assumption.

- Journal of Lightwave Technology, "Photonic Neural Networks: Principles,
  Progress, and Prospects" (2026).
  URL: https://opg.optica.org/jlt/abstract.cfm?URI=jlt-44-3-821
  Notes: Emphasizes that DAC, ADC, and transimpedance amplifier overheads can
  dominate practical photonic-neural-network energy.

- PMC / Nature, "Neuromorphic photonic computing with an electro-optic analog
  neuron" (2026).
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12992803/
  Notes: Notes the system-level cost of moving and converting data between
  digital and analog domains in neuromorphic photonic systems.

- Physical Review Applied, "Quantifying Power in Silicon Photonic Neural
  Networks" (2022).
  URL: https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevApplied.17.054029
  Notes: System-level power accounting reference for silicon photonic neural
  networks with state-of-the-art and future hardware assumptions.

- APL Photonics, "InP photonic integrated multi-layer neural networks" (2022).
  URL: https://pubs.aip.org/aip/app/article/7/1/010801/2835098/InP-photonic-integrated-multi-layer-neural
  Notes: Reports effective energy-per-MAC ranges for InP O/E/O and all-optical
  multilayer neural networks, including volatile weighting and nonvolatile
  weighting assumptions.

- arXiv / ACM ICS, "Mirage: An RNS-Based Photonic Accelerator for DNN Training"
  (2023/2025).
  URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17323v2
  Notes: Useful warning case for ADC/DAC energy, especially the claim that ADC
  energy can be orders of magnitude higher than DAC energy and can dominate
  analog/photonic MAC savings.

- MIT News, "Photonic processor could enable ultrafast AI computations with
  extreme energy efficiency" (2024).
  URL: https://news.mit.edu/2024/photonic-processor-could-enable-ultrafast-ai-computations-1202
  Notes: Reports an integrated photonic processor completing key deep-learning
  computations in less than half a nanosecond, supporting the optimistic latency
  bound only when computation remains optical and tightly integrated.

## 2026-06-22 Physical Device Tolerance Sources

- arXiv / IEEE, "Thermal Crosstalk Modelling and Compensation Methods for
  Programmable Photonic Integrated Circuits" (2024).
  URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10589v1
  Notes: Treats thermal crosstalk in programmable PICs as resonance-wavelength
  shifts in an MRR-like structure and reports sub-pm to low-pm modeling and
  compensation errors, making it a useful anchor for translating normalized
  drift into resonance-shift budgets.

- Nature Communications, "Quantum-limited stochastic optical neural networks
  operating at a few quanta per activation" (2025).
  URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55220-y
  Notes: Shows useful optical inference in extremely low-photon regimes with
  physics-aware stochastic training, while making the SNR/photon-count tradeoff
  explicit for detector-noise targets.

- arXiv / Optica, "Control-free and efficient integrated photonic neural
  networks via hardware-aware training and pruning" (2024).
  URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08180
  Notes: Reports that hardware-aware training/pruning can recover MRR-based PNN
  accuracy under disturbances without complex device control, including a
  reported handwritten-digit improvement from 67.0% to 95.0%.

- Nature Communications, "Physical neural networks using sharpness-aware
  training" (2026).
  URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68470-9
  Notes: Frames thermal drift, misalignment, fabrication variation, and
  post-deployment perturbations as deployment blockers for physical neural
  networks; reports that a 12 pm MRR resonance shift, about 0.2 C in their
  setup, can sharply degrade MNIST accuracy without robust training.

- arXiv, "Compact, Reconfigurable, and Scalable Photonic Neurons by
  Modulation-and-Weighting Microring Resonators" (2025/2026).
  URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11369v6
  Notes: Current MRR-neuron design note that uses TEC control, per-channel laser
  tuning, and periodic re-sweeping of current-weight maps to handle fabrication
  offsets and slow thermal drift.

- Nanophotonics, "Resolving the thermal challenges for silicon microring
  resonator devices" (2013).
  URL: https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/nanoph-2013-0013/html
  Notes: Background survey on silicon microring thermal susceptibility and
  stabilization/athermalization strategies; useful as a qualitative warning
  source even where publisher access is awkward.

- PMC, "Photonic and Thermal Modelling of Microrings in Silicon, Diamond and
  GaN" (2020).
  URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7279479/
  Notes: Reports modeled temperature-induced resonance shifts including
  68.2 pm/K for silicon, providing an order-of-magnitude anchor for thermal
  sensitivity.

## 2026-06-23 Converter Target Sources

- B. Murmann, "ADC Performance Survey 1997-2026."
  URL: https://github.com/bmurmann/ADC-survey
  Notes: Current public data collection for ISSCC and VLSI Circuit Symposium ADC
  performance. Use as the primary survey source when checking whether projected
  ADC energy targets are ordinary, aggressive, or fantasy.

- IEEE TCAS-I, "Low-Power SAR ADC Design: Overview and Survey of
  State-of-the-Art Techniques" (2022).
  URL: https://labs.engineering.asu.edu/mixedsignals/wp-content/uploads/sites/58/2023/04/Low-Power_SAR_ADC_Design_Overview_and_Survey_of_State-of-the-Art_Techniques-1.pdf
  Notes: Reports SAR ADC energy-efficiency improvement from about
  3 pJ/conversion-step in 2000 to 0.4 fJ/conversion-step in 2019, while making
  clear that conversion-step FoM is not the same as full sample energy.

- IEEE JSSC / Semantic Scholar, "A 24-72-GS/s 8-b Time-Interleaved SAR ADC
  With 2.0-3.3-pJ/Conversion and >30 dB SNDR at Nyquist in 14-nm CMOS FinFET"
  (2018).
  URL: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-24%E2%80%9372-GS-s-8-b-Time-Interleaved-SAR-ADC-With-and-Kull-Luu/7815a2dfb46434788fd31dc718425fcf2c99ed15
  Notes: Concrete high-speed ADC data point: 8-bit, 24-72 GS/s, 2.0-3.3 pJ per
  conversion. This supports using <= 5 pJ/output as plausible but demanding.

- Analog Integrated Circuits and Signal Processing, "Analysis of energy
  consumption bounds in CMOS current-steering digital-to-analog converters"
  (2022).
  URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10470-022-02013-2
  Notes: Open-access DAC energy-bound analysis. Useful for the DAC side of the
  target spec because it derives noise, speed, and linearity bounds rather than
  relying on one architecture point.

- APL Photonics, "InP photonic integrated multi-layer neural networks" (2022).
  URL: https://pubs.aip.org/aip/app/article/7/1/010801/2835098/InP-photonic-integrated-multi-layer-neural
  Notes: System-level photonic NN model uses 25 mW DACs, 25 mW ADCs, and 5 mW
  receiver PDs at 10 GHz transceiver speed, equivalent to about 2.5 pJ/sample
  for DAC/ADC and 0.5 pJ/sample for receiver power at that rate. Strong support
  for the shared-boundary target, weaker support for repeated per-block
  conversion.

- Electronics, "A 3GS/s 12-bit Current-Steering Digital-to-Analog Converter
  (DAC) in 55 nm CMOS Technology" (2019).
  URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/8/4/464
  Notes: Primary DAC linearity anchor. Reports a 12-bit 3 GS/s current-steering
  DAC with 495 mW total power, DNL -0.55/+1.21 LSB, INL -1.22/+0.64 LSB, and
  SFDR above 50 dBc across the Nyquist zone. Energy is too high for the optical
  target, but the source is useful for turning the missing DAC constraint into
  explicit ENOB/linearity fields.

- IEEE Photonics Technology Letters / IEEE Xplore, "Silicon Photonic
  Segmented Modulator-Based Electro-Optic DAC for 100 Gb/s PAM-4 Generation"
  (2015).
  URL: https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7185365
  Notes: Primary modulator-side caution. Demonstrates a 2-bit segmented
  electro-optic DAC using a multi-electrode silicon MZM, with the longer segment
  measured at 48 GHz electro-optic bandwidth. This supports the optical-DAC
  route for rate, but not the 7-8 bit effective precision needed by the transfer
  model.

- Journal of Semiconductors, "A survey of high-speed high-resolution current
  steering DACs" (2020).
  URL: https://doi.org/10.1088/1674-4926/41/11/111404
  Notes: Secondary synthesis used only to frame the DAC trade space: high-speed
  current-steering DACs are bounded by sampling rate, high-frequency linearity,
  static mismatch, finite output impedance, timing mismatch, switching
  transients, and calibration. The report should therefore carry linearity
  constraints alongside energy and sample rate.

## 2026-07-01 DAC Source-Chain Candidate Search

- Analog Devices, "AD9172: Dual, 16-Bit, 12.6 GSPS RF DAC with Channelizers."
  URL: https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ad9172.pdf
  Notes: Closest commercial DAC source-chain candidate found for rate and
  linearity. Supports 12.6 GSPS DAC update rate and lists 2.55 W at 12 GSPS in
  dual-channel mode; static INL/DNL are +/-7 LSB and ENOB is not specified.

- Analog Devices, "High Speed Converters Lead Industry with 28 nm CMOS
  Technology."
  URL: https://www.analog.com/media/en/news-marketing-collateral/product-highlight/ad9208-ad9172-high-speed-converters.pdf
  Notes: Product brief reports AD9172 performance at 12 GSPS and 1.8 GHz with
  70 dBc single-tone SFDR and 1.5 W/channel. This clears rate/SFDR but maps to
  about 125 pJ/sample/channel, about 50x above the 2.5 pJ/sample target.

- WCSE, "A 10GS/s 8-bit Current Steering DAC in 65nm CMOS Technology."
  URL: https://www.wcse.org/content-14-499-1.html
  Notes: Simulated 10 GS/s 8-bit DAC with 37.5 mW total power, about
  3.75 pJ/sample, but only up to 43 dB SFDR over the Nyquist bandwidth. Useful
  as an energy/rate near-miss, not a passing precision/linearity candidate.

- University of Stuttgart, "Digital-to-Analog-Converter with 100 GS/s."
  URL: https://www.int.uni-stuttgart.de/en/research/ic/dac100g/
  Notes: 100 GS/s 8-bit DAC reference for optical communications. Rate is ample,
  but ENOB/SFDR ranges only from 5.3 bit/41 dB down to 3.2 bit/27 dB from DC to
  24.9 GHz, and the chip consumes 2.5 W.

- Lucas Duncan, "A 10-bit DC-20 GHz Multiple-Return-To-Zero DAC with >48 dB
  SFDR."
  URL: https://rave.ohiolink.edu/etdc/view?acc_num=osu1492740839889776
  Notes: RF-DAC architecture reference with >48 dB SFDR from DC to 20 GHz, but
  its 3.35 GS/s sample rate and 1.91 W consumption miss the project target.
